NACA 0012 Airfoil Validation

The NACA 0012 is the most-tested airfoil there is, and NASA publishes it as a reference case for turbulence models. I ran a full lift polar from 0° to 18° at a Reynolds number of 6 million and compared every angle with wind-tunnel data and NASA's own CFD. Everything on this page is live data from the runs. Setup

AirfoilNASA TMR NACA 0012 (sharp trailing edge), chord 1 m, 2D
FlowRe = 6 × 10⁶, M = 0.15 run incompressible (51.04 m/s)
MeshStructured C-mesh written by my own Python script, 630 × 179 = 112,770 cells, farfield 500 chords
WallFirst cell 2 µm, y+ ≤ 1.05 at every angle (boundary layer resolved, no wall functions)
SolversimpleFoam (steady RANS), k-ω SST, SIMPLEC, second-order upwind
AngleSet by tilting the free stream, not the mesh (same mesh for every angle)
Run6000 iterations per angle, forces averaged over the last 500; 7–13 min on 4 cores

Validation

References: Ladson, NASA TM 4074 (1988); Gregory & O'Reilly, ARC R&M 3726 (1970); NASA Langley Turbulence Modeling Resource, case 2DN00 (CFL3D SST).

What I learned

Steady RANS doesn't stall

At 18° the experiment has stalled (Cl drops from 1.61 to 1.00), but my simulation keeps climbing to 1.60. The skin friction shows only a small reversed-flow patch at the trailing edge. This is a known limitation of steady SST: it keeps the flow attached too long. NASA's case stops at 15° for this reason. Drag is also 9–17 % high from 12° up, as NASA's own SST result is.

Meshing thin cells

My first meshes (blockMesh) all diverged. Cells near the wall are 2 µm tall and up to 1000× longer than that, and OpenFOAM only tolerates that shape if the cells are exact rectangles. A few percent of taper made them read as 85° skewed. I wrote my own mesh generator that keeps every wake cell rectangular, and it ran first time.

Don't stop on residuals alone

My first runs at 4°, 8° and 10° stopped early because the residuals met their targets, while lift was still creeping up. I removed the early stop and judge every run on its force history instead. The redone runs changed by less than 0.6 %, but the rule is now safer.
